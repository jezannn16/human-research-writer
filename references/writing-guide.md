# Writing, Argumentation and Tables

## Chapter Preparation

Before writing, create a short map: thesis → specific evidence → limitation → implications for the chapter's objective. Then write a coherent text based on this map. Provide the user with the finished text and the necessary confirmations; Show your service card only upon request. This approach is chosen in a local comparison of three methods over five short runs; universal superiority over ordinary writing has not been established.

The chapter plan should solve its problem in the coursework. In the theoretical chapter, compare approaches and reasons for differences; in practical - explain the method, the origin of the data, calculations and limitations. Don't apply the same structure to all disciplines.

Working connection: research question → items or data under consideration → analysis → conclusion relevant to the problem. This is a guideline for the argument, not necessarily the same pattern in every paragraph. If a topic requires a definition, first explain it by source, then use the term consistently.

## Natural academic Russian

Write in such a way that the reader understands what exactly has been established and on what basis. Favor concrete action over empty evaluation: instead of “the issue is extremely important,” explain what result or controversy is being considered. Use a professional term if it is more accurate than a common word; explain it at the first meaningful mention.

Connect sentences with logic rather than constant “furthermore,” “thus,” and “it should be noted.” Leave a transition when it clarifies a causal relationship, opposition, or the boundary of a conclusion. Specific user restrictions on characters and empty patterns are specified in [strict profile](style-profile.md); keep them. Don't mechanically ban other common words and don't replace them with random synonyms.

Vary the length of sentences and paragraphs according to the complexity of the idea. Remove repeated announcement of the topic and retelling of what was just said. Maintain necessary explanations and explicit conclusions: academic clarity is more important than trying to make the text unusual.

Distinguish:
- the author of the source approves or receives the result;
- the data show an observable relationship;
- the coursework author offers an interpretation;
- There is not enough information to make a conclusion.

Do not depict a student's experiment, interview, practice, or personal experience without supporting evidence. Do not add intentional errors, conversational insertions, random non-linearity and artificial “non-ideality”. “Human language” here means clarity and meaningfulness, not a promise to hide the origin of the text.

If the user has provided a sample of their own text, adjust the clarity, degree of formality and familiar terminology, while maintaining the accuracy and requirements of the manual.

## Sources within the chapter

Every externally verifiable statement needs traceable support. Statements of purpose, chapter organization, and own analytical transitions do not require fictitious external references; grounds for your own conclusions are required.

For each source, retain bibliographic details, URL/DOI or path to the provided file, access date for modified web data, version, location of acknowledgment, and associated statements. If material is available only as an abstract, use only what is supported by the abstract and do not attribute conclusions of the full text to it.

Prepare your bibliography according to a manual or a clearly defined style. If the style is not specified, use sequential links [1] and available details; do not assert compliance with a specific GOST without checking the requirements. A quote requires precise wording and a proven locator. Distinguish between PDF page number and printed numbering; For HTML, a section title and a persistent fragment are suitable. Don't make up the page number.

Repeated links lead to the same post rather than creating a duplicate. A source that has just been found, but has not yet been read or verified, remains a candidate, and not the support of a ready-made statement.

## Tables and calculations

Create a table upon request or when it makes the actual comparison clearer. First, determine the meaning of the rows and columns. Include title, units, period, and data area when applicable. If the row sources differ, show this in a column or note.

Before the table, explain its purpose, after - interpret the significant result without retelling all the cells. The rows must be comparable in definition, period, and units. Express the difference in percentages in percentage points; consider the relative change from an explicitly specified base.

Separate the original values from the calculated ones. Show the formula, denominator, rounding, and rules for handling omissions when they affect the result. A zero denominator means an unspecified relative change, not 0%. Do not replace a blank with a zero; Don't add up incompatible units.

Conditional numbers are only acceptable as an explicitly designated teaching example. If you need real results, but there are none, offer an empty table form or request specific data. The blank form and synthetic example cannot be described as the results of the research.

When adding a table to an existing chapter, update the references and numbering without changing unaffected parts. Any new factual content and calculation will be audited by Luna. Verified content can be reused in another format, but you need to check its transfer: numbers, notes, sources and captions.

## Format and continuity

Before issuing, check each requested section for compliance with its volume and composition, separately from the total amount of work. For the word limit, consider the main text of the section without the title and bibliography, unless the manual specifies a different base. If pages are required, check the actual layout rather than equating a page to a constant number of words. Correct shortfalls and excesses before issuing.

User format preferences are subject to the capabilities of the environment. For DOCX, PDF, tables and other files, find a suitable available tool or skill; the text skill itself does not create files of these formats. Check the saved file and, for a completed document, its visual representation.

If the format is not available, say so and offer editable text or another available format. With a large coursework, do not call the first part a complete work. In the status register, note what is completed and what remains. When continuing, read the previously saved work and take into account the user’s comments.
